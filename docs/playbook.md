# Investigation playbook

How a signal becomes a lead a rep can call. Learned by working eight real
cases by hand on 2026-10-01 (four Minneapolis permits, four news headlines).

## The lead bar

A lead goes to the rep only when it has all of these:

1. **The business**, meaning the tenant or operator. Not the contractor, not the landlord.
2. **Local or small regional.** National chains and their franchisees run on national
   telecom deals. Enterprises go to another sales team.
3. **Before or just after opening.** Pre-opening is best. "Opened last week" usually
   means service is already installed.
4. **Inside the territory**, confirmed by street address, not by city name.
5. **A named person** (owner, founder, operations) and at least one business channel
   (phone or email).
6. **Why now**, in one sentence with a source link.

Anything missing a field goes on a watchlist, not to the rep.

## What eight cases showed

| Source | Cases | Good leads | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permits | 4 | 2, plus 1 already open | Strong signal; needs investigation to name the tenant |
| News | 4 | 0 | Two were other states (Blaine WA, Hudson NY); two had already opened |

Permits are the backbone. News is corroboration at best.

## Triage before spending any effort

- **News: check the state first.** Syndicated headlines ("Blaine", "Hudson") are often
  other states. Read the outlet and URL, not the search engine's summary.
- **News: future tense only.** "Preparing to open", "plans", "coming soon",
  "under construction", "eyes ... opening". Skip "opens" and "opened".
- **Skip national chains and franchises**, and tenants inside a host building.
- **Permits: look for a new occupant.** Tenant improvement, "new [use]", certificate of
  occupancy required, a distinctive project name. Skip trade-only work on an occupied
  building (HVAC, roof, windows, repairs).

## Permit to named business

In order, stopping as soon as the business is named:

1. **Quote any distinctive name on the permit** (project title, or the applicant LLC if it
   is not a holding company). Fastest path: one or two searches.
2. **Quote the street address.** Gives the building name, the landlord, current and
   previous tenants.
3. **Search the use plus neighborhood plus year** ("indoor soccer facility Northeast
   Minneapolis 2026"). Tenant trackers, landlord press releases and realtor blogs carry
   these.
4. **Cross-check against the businesses Places lists at the address** and any matching
   headline. Three sources agreeing is a confirmed tenant.

If four searches name nothing, stop and recheck in two to four weeks. A food license,
signage permit or "coming soon" listing usually appears by then.

## Named business to named person

1. "[business] founder" or "owner". Best sources: crowdfunding pages, landlord press
   releases, news articles, BBB profiles.
2. "[domain] contact email phone". Search snippets often carry the site footer.

## Cost per case

Three to ten searches. A distinctive name resolves in about six. A generic use with no
press does not resolve and should be rechecked later, not chased.

## Missing tools, in order of value

1. **Minnesota Secretary of State business search**: new LLCs at an address, with
   organizer and registered agent. Would have named the one case that failed.
2. **Full permit detail from the city portal**: suite number and applicant contact.
3. **Reading full pages.** Every case here worked from search snippets only.

## Contact rules

Business sources only: company sites, news, landlord releases, public filings,
business listings. No people-search or data-broker sites. No personal cell numbers or
home addresses, even when a snippet shows one.
